# SummitShare Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

SummitShare brings people together to preserve, share, and support the return of African cultural heritage. Everyone who participates deserves respect and a community free from harassment.

We welcome people of every age, disability, ethnicity, nationality, race, caste, religion, gender identity and expression, sexual orientation, socioeconomic background, and level of experience. These expectations apply equally to contributors, maintainers, community members, and project representatives.

## Expected behavior

- Treat people with dignity, listen carefully, and make room for different perspectives.
- Give constructive feedback about ideas and work. Disagree without personal attacks.
- Welcome newcomers and explain unfamiliar concepts patiently.
- Respect personal boundaries, privacy, and requests to stop unwanted contact.
- Take responsibility for mistakes, acknowledge their impact, and help repair harm.
- Credit other people's work and be honest about your contributions, affiliations, and conflicts of interest.

## Respect for cultural heritage

Our work involves living communities, sensitive histories, and cultural knowledge. Participants must:

- Listen to communities of origin and respect how people describe their own identities, histories, and traditions.
- Respect community protocols and permissions when sharing cultural material. Do not publish sacred, restricted, or private knowledge without appropriate authorization from its custodians.
- Represent sources, provenance, and permissions accurately. Clearly distinguish documented facts from interpretations and uncertainty.
- Discuss colonialism, repatriation, and historical harm with care. Good-faith disagreement is welcome; demeaning people or cultures is not.
- Avoid claiming to speak for a community or institution without its authorization.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- Harassment, discrimination, hateful language, slurs, or degrading stereotypes.
- Threats, intimidation, stalking, or encouragement of violence.
- Sexual harassment, unwelcome sexual attention, or sexual content directed at participants without their consent.
- Insults, targeted humiliation, trolling, or sustained disruption of discussions and activities.
- Sharing someone's private information without permission, including linking their identity to a wallet address without consent.
- Impersonation, scams, or deceptive requests for funds, credentials, private keys, or seed phrases.
- Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith, supports a report, or participates in a review.
- Evading moderation restrictions or encouraging others to violate this code.

## Where this applies

This code applies in SummitShare-managed repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions, community channels, meetings, and events. It also applies when someone officially represents SummitShare in public.

Conduct outside these spaces may be considered when it directly affects the safety or participation of people in the SummitShare community, such as harassment of a contributor through private messages about project work.

## Reporting a concern

Report concerns to the SummitShare building team at [info@summitshare.co](mailto:info@summitshare.co), using the subject **Code of Conduct report**. You may report something you experienced or witnessed. You do not need to confront the person involved before reporting.

Share only what you feel comfortable providing. Helpful details include:

- What happened, where, and approximately when.
- Relevant usernames, links, messages, or screenshots.
- Whether the behavior is ongoing and any immediate concerns.
- How you would prefer to be contacted and any support you need.

Do not post sensitive reports in public issues or pull requests. The reporting address is the project's existing general contact inbox, so its recipients may see your message. You may first request a private conversation with an uninvolved maintainer before sharing sensitive details.

Reports will be handled discreetly, with information shared only as needed to assess the concern and respond. Complete confidentiality cannot be guaranteed. A report made in good faith is not misconduct merely because it cannot be substantiated.

## Response and enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for applying this code fairly, including when a concern involves another maintainer. Anyone involved in a report or with a conflict of interest must step aside from reviewing it and deciding the outcome.

Reviewers will consider the available information, the impact on participants, the severity of the behavior, and any relevant pattern of previous conduct. They may seek clarification and give the people involved an opportunity to respond when doing so is safe. Temporary restrictions may be used while a concern is reviewed.

Depending on the circumstances, action may include:

1. A request to stop the behavior or correct harmful content.
2. A written warning with clear expectations for future participation.
3. Removal of content or a temporary restriction from project spaces or roles.
4. A permanent ban from project spaces or removal from a project role.

These actions are not a required sequence. Serious or repeated misconduct may lead directly to a ban. Maintainers should explain decisions and any restrictions to affected participants while protecting other people's private information, and update the reporter on the outcome to the extent appropriate.

## Requesting a review

If you believe a decision was mistaken or relevant information was missed, contact [info@summitshare.co](mailto:info@summitshare.co) with the subject **Code of Conduct review** and explain your concern. Where an uninvolved maintainer is available, they should review the request. If independent review is unavailable, the team should make that limitation clear. Existing restrictions remain in place during review unless the team explicitly changes them.
